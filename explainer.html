Is it real? · NBA team stats, 2015-16 to 2024-25

Signal, Noise and k

How many games before a team's numbers mean something, why that question has a one-number answer, and how to turn a hot start into an estimate you'd bet on.

Motivation

One game is three times as noisy as the gap between teams

Every stat you see after N games is true level + noise. For point margin, the noise in a single game has a standard deviation of 13.9 points. The true strength of NBA teams, best to worst, spreads only 4.7 points (SD). So a single result tells you almost nothing about which team you watched, and averaging is the only way out.

Noise, defined. Take one team-season, subtract that team's own season-average margin from each of its 82 game margins, and take the SD of those 82 residuals; pool across all team-seasons. That is σe = 13.9. It is not the SD of all game margins in the league (≈ 14.7), which also contains the differences between teams. Signal, σT = 4.7, is the SD of season-average margins across teams after removing the part of their spread that is itself sampling noise: observed SD of the 30 season means is 4.94 (averaged over the 8 seasons), but an 82-game mean carries noise variance σe²/82 = 13.9²/82 = 2.37, so σT² = 4.94² − 2.37 = 22.1 and σT = 4.7.

Calculation

k: the exchange rate between games and knowledge

Take a team's average margin over its first n games. Two things are mixed into that number: the team's true level, which varies between teams with SD σT = 4.7, and leftover noise, which averaging shrinks from 13.9 to 13.9/√n (after 7 games 5.3, after 30 games 2.5). Line the 30 teams up by their n-game averages: some of their spread is real, some is leftover noise. Reliability is the real share:

rel(n) = σT² / (σT² + σe²/n)  =  n / (n + k),   with   k = σe² / σT²
n = 7: true variance 22.1, leftover noise variance 194/7 = 27.7 → rel = 22.1 / (22.1 + 27.7) = 0.44
same thing via k: 7 / (7 + 8.8) = 0.44. Dividing top and bottom by σT² is all that happened.

k is measured in games. It is the number of games of a team's own data that carry exactly as much information as the fact that it is an NBA team, i.e. as the league's distribution of strength. At n = k you are 50/50; at n = 2.3k you are 70% signal; at n = 9k, 90%.

Method 1 · moments

8.8

Pooled within-season variance of single-game margins (noise) divided by the between-team variance of season means minus its own sampling share (signal).

Method 2 · split-half

8.5

Shuffle each season's 82 games into two halves of 41, correlate the two half-season means across teams (r = 0.83), invert r = 41/(41+k).

Method 3 · curve fit

11.7

Fit k to the real first-N-vs-rest curve. It comes out higher than the other two because teams drift within a season (trades, rest, tanking); the stationary model is slightly too optimistic.

Interpretation

Nin and Nout: the same curve, measured honestly

Narsu's 2017 table asked: after how many games does the correlation between a team's stats-so-far and its final stats pass r² = 0.5? That is Nin. But the final number contains the first N games, so the correlation is inflated and reaches 1 at N = 82 whatever the stat. Nout correlates the first N games with the remaining games instead.

Why Nout is often a dash

The best any split can do is half-season vs half-season: r²max = (41/(41+k))². That exceeds 0.5 only when k < 17. For 3PT% (k = 48) or Def Rtg (k = 19) no N ever qualifies. The dash means the between-team spread is small relative to game noise, not that the stat is useless.

Read k instead of a threshold

0.5 (Narsu) and 0.7 (Blackport) are conventions. k converts any threshold into games: games for reliability t = k·t/(1−t). Nin itself is k·41/(41+k) under the stationary model, so Nin and k carry the same information, and k is the one you can also use to shrink.

Using it

A prior is a measurement worth K games

Shrinkage pools two measurements of the same thing by their precision: this season's average over n games, and a prior guess μ whose error has SD τ.

estimate = (n · x̄ + K · μ) / (n + K),   K = σe² / τ²

An n-game average has sampling variance σe²/n. A prior with error variance τ² is therefore exactly as informative as K = σe²/τ² games. "Worth 14 games" means: the prior's error SD equals the noise of a 14-game average. The plain k is the special case where the prior is the league itself (μ = league mean, τ = σT).

League mean

μ = 0, τ = σT. Assumes nothing about the team. Compresses everyone toward average but keeps this season's order. The padding in the 2026-11-04 report, §3.5.

Last season, raw

μ = last season's value, τ = σT by choice. A half-measure: it uses last year's number but admits as much uncertainty as knowing nothing, so it gets the same K as the league prior.

Last season, regressed

μ = 0.58 × last season, τ = 3.8. What last year actually predicts about this year, fitted on ten season pairs. Worth 14 games, so after 7 games it still holds 67% of the weight.

Your projection

Any pre-season number (a model, a market line) with its own K = σe²/τ². A line with error SD 3.5 is worth about 16 games. Supply it as --prior-csv.

All four give the team's current true level, not a season total. A projected final = (n·raw + (82−n)·estimate)/82.

A test of the method

Quarters: more observations, not more information

Splitting a game into four quarters quadruples the rows but not the possessions, and the pieces are not equal. Signal per quarter falls through the game; the "fourth-quarter team" deviation is weakly real, and mostly a side-effect of big leads and bench minutes.
